# Pipeline run: one package through the gauntlet

An annotated transcript of a single application package moving through the [Careers pipeline](../docs/PIPELINE.md), sanitized. The company and role are invented (a mid-market SaaS company hiring a Chief of Staff), but the shape of the run is representative: this one includes a truthfulness kickback and a QC kickback, because a run with no kickbacks would tell you nothing about whether the gates work. Roles below are the functions from [the agent catalog](../docs/AGENT-CATALOG.md).

## 1 · The positioning brief

The Strategy Lead reads the job description and writes the brief every downstream agent works from.

> **Lead with:** operator variant. This JD says "Chief of Staff" but reads like "head of internal operations": three of five bullets are about running the operating cadence, not advising the CEO.
> **Top themes:** cross-functional program delivery, executive communication, comfort with ambiguity in a 200-person company.
> **Hidden priority:** the line "you will own our planning process" plus a recent Series C suggests the real job is building the first real planning function. Emphasize zero-to-one process work.
> **Honest stretch:** the JD asks for SaaS revenue-operations exposure. The candidate has adjacent program work, not direct RevOps ownership. Do not paper over this.

## 2 · The tailor's draft

The Résumé Tailor mirrors JD language where truthful. One draft bullet, as written:

> Drove 65% revenue growth by leading the go-to-market launch of a flagship platform product across three regions.

## 3 · Truthfulness kickback

The Achievement Verifier audits every claim against the achievement bank. It is a hard gate; no other agent can override it.

> **KICKBACK · bullet 2, current role.** Flagged claim: "Drove 65% revenue growth."
> The bank supports: candidate ran the launch program (timeline, cross-team coordination, executive reporting) for the product whose category grew 65% that year. Attribution of the growth number to the candidate is borrowed pedigree. In an interview, "walk me through how you personally drove that 65%" is unanswerable as written.
> **Required rewrite:** credit the program, hedge the outcome. Resubmit.

The corrected bullet, accepted on the second pass:

> Led the cross-regional launch program for a flagship platform product, coordinating 6 teams to an on-time release in a line of business that grew 65% that year.

Same facts, survivable under cross-examination.

## 4 · The recruiter simulator's fifteen seconds

The Recruiter Simulator reads the finished package the way a busy recruiter does, hunting for reasons to put it down.

> **First impression: 7/10.** Page one lands the operator story.
> **Most likely rejection:** title mismatch. The last two roles say "Program Lead" and the JD wants Chief of Staff; a skimming recruiter may bucket this as too junior before reaching the summary. Mitigation: the summary's first line must claim the CoS scope explicitly, and it currently buries it in sentence three.
> **Strongest signal:** the planning-cycle bullet. It maps one-to-one to the JD's "own our planning process" line.

The summary was reordered in response. The simulator reports; it never edits.

## 5 · Humanization pass

The Humanization Editor rewrites anything that reads machine-made. One before/after:

> **Before:** Leveraged cross-functional collaboration to streamline operational workflows, resulting in enhanced organizational efficiency.
> **After:** Cut the weekly ops review from 90 minutes to 40 by killing three redundant status reports and making the remaining one actually load-bearing.

The first version passes every keyword check and says nothing. The second is a specific claim a human would make, with a number the Verifier had already cleared.

## 6 · QC gate summary

The QC Reviewer runs the full checklist pass over the rendered files.

> **Gates: 21 of 22 pass.**
> Structure, ATS coverage (91%), format adherence, voice, house style: pass.
> **Fail, gate 14 (cross-document consistency):** cover letter says "seven years leading programs," résumé dates support six and a half. Kicked back to the Cover-Letter Writer with instructions: use "six-plus years" or restructure the sentence.
> **Re-run after fix: 22 of 22 pass.**

Minor issues get fixed in place; this one crossed documents, so it went back to the named author with notes. That is the loop working, not the loop failing.

## 7 · Committee

The Committee Chair makes the final holistic call, after the checklists, never instead of them.

> **GO.** The package tells one coherent story (operator who builds planning functions) instead of three partial ones, and every number in it survives the follow-up question.

## 8 · What happens next

Nothing, until a person acts. The package lands in the review queue with its files rendered on disk. A human opens the portal, reads the package, and clicks submit, or doesn't. The pipeline's output is confidence, not a submission. The system prepares; the human decides.
